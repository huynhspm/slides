1.
Invertible Models

The Goal

Get an exact likelihood and fast sampling from the same model.
  • Autoregressive: exact likelihood, but slow sampling
  • VAE: fast sampling, but only a bound
  • Flows aim for both at once

Invertible Transformations

$$ x = f_\theta(z), \qquad z = f_\theta^{-1}(x) $$
  • \( f_\theta \) must be a bijection
  • Both directions must be computable
  • This forces \( z \) and \( x \) to have the same dimension

Change of Variables

$$ p_x(x) = p_z\big(f^{-1}(x)\big) \left| \det \frac{\partial f^{-1}}{\partial x} \right| $$
  • Exactly the identity from Lecture 02
  • The Jacobian determinant accounts for local volume change
  • Everything is exact — no bound anywhere

The Log-Likelihood

$$ \log p_x(x) = \log p_z(z) + \log \left| \det \frac{\partial f^{-1}}{\partial x} \right| $$
  • Directly maximisable by gradient descent
  • The difficulty is entirely in that determinant

The Determinant Bottleneck

  • A general \( D \times D \) determinant costs \( O(D^3) \)
  • For a \( 256\times256\times3 \) image, \( D \approx 200{,}000 \)
  • So we must design transforms with cheap determinants

2.
Coupling Layers

Triangular Jacobians

For a triangular matrix, \( \det = \prod_i J_{ii} \) — an \( O(D) \) product of the diagonal.
  • The whole field is built on engineering this structure
  • Restrict each dimension to depend only on earlier ones

Coupling Layers

$$ y_{1:d} = x_{1:d}, \qquad y_{d+1:D} = x_{d+1:D} \odot \exp(s(x_{1:d})) + t(x_{1:d}) $$
  • Split the input; leave one half untouched
  • Transform the other half conditioned on the first
  • \( s \) and \( t \) can be arbitrary neural networks

Why Coupling Works

$$ \log\left|\det J\right| = \sum_j s_j(x_{1:d}) $$
  • The Jacobian is triangular by construction
  • The determinant is just a sum of the scale outputs
  • Inversion is a simple algebraic rearrangement

Alternating the Split

  • One coupling layer leaves half the input unchanged
  • Permute the split between layers so everything gets transformed
  • Depth compensates for each layer's restricted form

3.
RealNVP and Glow

RealNVP

  • Stacks affine coupling layers with alternating masks
  • Checkerboard and channel-wise masking patterns
  • Multi-scale architecture: factor out dimensions as depth increases

Glow

  • Adds invertible 1×1 convolutions as learned permutations
  • Actnorm replaces batch normalisation
  • Demonstrated high-resolution faces with semantic latent editing

Sampling

$$ z \sim p_z(z), \qquad x = f_\theta(z) $$
  • A single forward pass — no sequential loop, no iteration
  • Genuinely fast at generation time

4.
Trade-offs

Strengths

  • Exact likelihood — not a bound
  • Fast one-pass sampling
  • Exact latent inference by running the model backwards
  • Stable maximum-likelihood training

Limitations

  • The latent must have the same dimension as the data
  • Invertibility restricts the architecture severely
  • Very deep stacks are needed, so memory cost is high
  • Sample quality lags GANs and diffusion

Comparing the Three Families So Far

  • Autoregressive: exact likelihood, slow sampling
  • VAE: bound only, fast sampling, compressed latent
  • Flows: exact likelihood, fast sampling, no compression

Where Flows Reappear

  • The continuous-time view leads to neural ODEs
  • That path arrives at flow matching in Lecture 15
  • The transport idea outlives this particular architecture

Summary

  • Flows model data as an invertible map from a simple density
  • The change-of-variables formula gives an exact likelihood
  • The Jacobian determinant must be cheap — hence coupling layers
  • RealNVP and Glow scale the idea to images
  • Exact likelihood and fast sampling, paid for with architectural rigidity
