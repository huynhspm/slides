1.
The Energy Perspective

A Different Starting Point

Instead of a normalised density, assign every \( x \) a scalar energy: low for plausible data, high for implausible.
  • No architectural restriction at all
  • Any network that outputs one number will do

The Boltzmann Distribution

$$ p_\theta(x) = \frac{\exp(-E_\theta(x))}{Z_\theta}, \qquad Z_\theta = \int \exp(-E_\theta(x))\, dx $$
  • Low energy → high probability
  • \( Z_\theta \) is the partition function
  • It makes the density integrate to one

The Partition Function Problem

  • \( Z_\theta \) is an integral over all possible images
  • Intractable for any interesting model
  • So we can compare \( p(x_1) \) and \( p(x_2) \), but never state either

Unnormalized Freedom

  • Flows must be invertible; autoregressive models must be causal
  • An EBM has no such constraint
  • Maximum flexibility, paid for with an intractable normaliser

2.
Training Without Z

The Likelihood Gradient

$$ \nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(x) = -\nabla_\theta E_\theta(x) + \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(x')}\big[\nabla_\theta E_\theta(x')\big] $$
  • The first term uses real data — easy
  • The second is an expectation under the model — hard
  • Note that \( Z_\theta \) itself has vanished

Push Down, Push Up

  • Positive phase: lower the energy of observed data
  • Negative phase: raise the energy of model samples
  • Training sculpts an energy landscape around the data

Contrastive Divergence

  • We need model samples \( x' \sim p_\theta \) at every gradient step
  • Run a short MCMC chain instead of a converged one
  • Biased but workable — the practical compromise

3.
MCMC and Langevin Dynamics

Markov Chain Monte Carlo

  • Build a chain whose stationary distribution is \( p_\theta \)
  • Each step needs only unnormalized density ratios
  • So \( Z_\theta \) never has to be computed

Langevin Dynamics

$$ x_{t+1} = x_t - \frac{\eta}{2}\nabla_x E_\theta(x_t) + \sqrt{\eta}\, \epsilon_t, \qquad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) $$
  • Gradient descent on energy, plus noise
  • The noise prevents collapse onto a single minimum
  • As \( \eta \to 0 \) and \( t \to \infty \) it samples \( p_\theta \) exactly

The Score Function

$$ \nabla_x \log p_\theta(x) = -\nabla_x E_\theta(x) $$
  • \( Z_\theta \) is constant in \( x \), so it disappears under \( \nabla_x \)
  • The score is computable even though the density is not
  • Remember this identity — Lecture 13 is built on it

Why Sampling Is Slow

  • Chains need many steps to mix across modes
  • Poor mixing gives biased gradients, which give a poor model
  • This is the practical ceiling on EBM training

4.
The Bridge to Diffusion

EBMs and Score Models

  • An EBM learns energy; its gradient is the score
  • Score models learn \( \nabla_x \log p(x) \) directly
  • Doing so skips both \( Z_\theta \) and the MCMC training loop

From Langevin to Diffusion

  • Langevin sampling walks noise toward the data using the score
  • Diffusion models learn that score at many noise levels
  • Annealing the noise fixes exactly the mixing problem above

Where EBMs Appear Today

  • Rarely as a standalone image generator
  • The perspective is what endures
  • Guidance in diffusion is an energy-based correction to the score

Summary

  • EBMs define \( p_\theta(x) \propto \exp(-E_\theta(x)) \) with no architectural constraints
  • The partition function \( Z_\theta \) is intractable
  • Training contrasts real data against model samples drawn by MCMC
  • Langevin dynamics samples using only the score
  • The score identity is the direct bridge to diffusion models
