1.
The Forward Process

Generation as Iterative Denoising

Destroying an image with noise is easy. If we can learn to undo one small step of it, we can generate.
  • No adversarial game, no invertibility constraint, no sequential ordering
  • Break one impossible jump into many easy steps

Two Processes

  • Forward: gradually add noise until only noise remains — fixed, no learning
  • Reverse: gradually remove noise to recover data — learned

The Forward Process

$$ q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1}, \beta_t I\big) $$
  • A small amount of Gaussian noise at each of \( T \) steps
  • \( \beta_t \) is the noise schedule
  • Scaling by \( \sqrt{1-\beta_t} \) keeps the variance bounded

The Closed Form

$$ q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t) I\big), \qquad \bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1-\beta_s) $$
  • Jump to any timestep in one shot
  • No need to simulate the chain during training
  • This is what makes training cheap

Reparameterized

$$ x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\, \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) $$
  • A noisy sample is just a weighted blend of data and noise
  • As \( \bar{\alpha}_t \to 0 \), \( x_T \) becomes pure noise

Noise Schedules

  • Linear: \( \beta_t \) increases linearly — the original choice
  • Cosine: destroys information more gradually, better for images
  • The schedule decides how model capacity is spent across timesteps

2.
The Reverse Process

The Reverse Process

$$ p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(x_t, t), \Sigma_\theta(x_t,t)\big) $$
  • If \( \beta_t \) is small, the true reverse step is also Gaussian
  • So we only need to predict its mean
  • One shared network handles every timestep, conditioned on \( t \)

The Tractable Posterior

$$ q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_{t-1}; \tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0), \tilde{\beta}_t I\big) $$
  • Conditioned on \( x_0 \), the reverse step is available in closed form
  • This gives us a target for the network to match

The Variational Bound

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_q\Big[\sum_{t>1} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) \,\|\, p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)\big)\Big] + \ldots $$
  • The same ELBO machinery from Lecture 03
  • A sum of KLs between Gaussians — each in closed form
  • A diffusion model is a deep hierarchical VAE with a fixed encoder

The Simplification

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon}\Big[\big\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \big\|^2 \Big] $$
  • Dropping the weights reduces everything to predicting the noise
  • Plain mean-squared error — remarkably stable
  • And empirically it produces better samples than the full bound

Training in Four Lines

  • Sample \( x_0 \) from the data and \( t \) uniformly
  • Sample \( \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) \), form \( x_t \) in closed form
  • Predict \( \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \)
  • Take a gradient step on the squared error

3.
Parameterization

Three Equivalent Targets

  • \( \epsilon \)-prediction: predict the noise — the standard choice
  • \( x_0 \)-prediction: predict the clean image directly
  • \( v \)-prediction: a blend of the two, more stable at high noise

Why They Are Equivalent

$$ \hat{x}_0 = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} $$
  • Given \( x_t \), each target determines the others
  • The choice changes the loss weighting, not the model class
  • \( v \)-prediction reappears in distillation in Lecture 18

The Network

  • A U-Net: encoder, bottleneck, decoder with skip connections
  • Skips preserve fine detail that the bottleneck would lose
  • The timestep \( t \) enters as a sinusoidal embedding
  • Self-attention at the lower resolutions

4.
Sampling

Ancestral Sampling

$$ x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_t}}\left(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}}\, \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\right) + \sigma_t z $$
  • Start from \( x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) \)
  • Apply this update for \( t = T, \ldots, 1 \)
  • Add fresh noise \( z \) at every step except the last

The Cost

  • DDPM used \( T = 1000 \) — that is 1000 forward passes per image
  • Orders of magnitude slower than a GAN
  • Lecture 13 attacks precisely this problem

Why Diffusion Won

  • Stable — one regression loss, no adversarial game
  • Diverse — likelihood-based training covers the modes
  • Scalable — quality improves predictably with compute
  • Controllable — conditioning slots in naturally

Summary

  • The forward process adds noise on a fixed schedule and has a closed form
  • The reverse process is learned, one Gaussian step at a time
  • The ELBO reduces to a simple noise-prediction MSE
  • \( \epsilon \)-, \( x_0 \)- and \( v \)-prediction are reparameterizations of one another
  • Sampling is stable and diverse, but takes hundreds of steps
