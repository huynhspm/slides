1.
Efficient Generation

The Cost of Iterative Sampling

$$ \text{cost} \approx \text{NFE} \times \text{cost per forward pass} $$
  • NFE — number of function evaluations
  • Diffusion needs 20–50; a GAN needs 1
  • This number dominates deployment cost

Reducing the Step Count

  • Better solvers (Lecture 13) — 1000 steps down to ~20
  • Straighter paths (Lecture 15) — down to 1–4
  • Distillation — train a student to take bigger steps

Progressive Distillation

  • Train a student to reproduce two teacher steps in one
  • Halve the step count, then repeat
  • 1024 → 512 → … → 4 steps, one halving at a time

Consistency Models

$$ f_\theta(x_t, t) = x_0 \quad \text{for every } t \text{ on the same trajectory} $$
  • Learn to jump to the endpoint from any point on the path
  • Enables genuine one-step generation
  • Trainable by distillation or from scratch

Cheaper Forward Passes

  • Quantization — 8-bit or 4-bit weights
  • Attention kernels — memory-efficient implementations
  • Distilled backbones — fewer parameters
  • Orthogonal to reducing NFE — the two multiply

2.
Deployment

Serving Constraints

  • GPU memory — model weights plus activations
  • Latency — time to first image
  • Throughput — images per second across users
  • Optimising one usually costs another

Batching

  • Diffusion batches well: every request runs the same step count
  • Larger batches raise throughput and also raise latency
  • Continuous batching keeps the GPU busy across arrivals

Resolution and Cost

  • Cost grows roughly with pixel count
  • Latent diffusion already bought a large constant factor
  • Generate small, then upscale with a cheaper specialised model

A Practical Checklist

  • Measure NFE before optimising anything else
  • Profile before you quantize
  • Cache text embeddings for repeated prompts
  • Decide the quality floor before tuning for speed

3.
Safety and Responsibility

Representation Bias

  • Training data reflects who took and captioned the photographs
  • Occupation prompts reproduce and often amplify stereotypes
  • Filtering changes the bias rather than removing it

Harmful Content

  • Explicit, violent and hateful imagery
  • Mitigations: dataset filtering, safety classifiers, concept erasure
  • All are partial, and all can be circumvented

Deepfakes and Consent

  • Photorealistic images of real, identifiable people
  • Non-consensual imagery is the most acute documented harm
  • Detection is a losing arms race against generation

Privacy and Memorisation

  • Models can memorise and reproduce training images
  • More likely for duplicated examples
  • Deduplication reduces but does not eliminate the risk

Copyright and Provenance

  • Training data is largely scraped without explicit licence
  • Style imitation sits in unsettled legal territory
  • Watermarking and signed content credentials aim to mark provenance
  • Robust invisible watermarking remains unsolved

4.
Open Problems & Closing

Open Problems: Control

  • Compositional prompts still fail: counting, spatial relations, attribute binding
  • Consistency of a character across images
  • Precise editing without disturbing the rest of the image

Open Problems: Scale and Fidelity

  • Native high-resolution generation without an upscaling cascade
  • Long-form video with temporal coherence
  • 3D generation limited by scarce training data

Open Problems: Foundations

  • Evaluation that actually tracks human judgement
  • Truly single-step sampling at full quality
  • Data efficiency — learning from far less
  • Attribution and provenance that survive editing

The Course, in One Question

How should we represent, learn, evaluate and sample from \( p_\theta(x) \)?
  • Every family we studied is one answer to this question
  • Autoregressive, latent, flow, adversarial, energy, diffusion, transport
  • They differ in assumptions and computational strategy, not in purpose

What to Take Away

  • Represent: explicitly, implicitly, or through a score or vector field
  • Learn: by likelihood, a bound, an adversary, or regression
  • Evaluate: never with a single number
  • Sample: in one step or many — this decides the cost

Summary

  • Sampling cost is NFE times cost per pass; attack both
  • Distillation and consistency models enable few-step generation
  • Deployment balances memory, latency and throughput
  • Bias, deepfakes, memorisation and provenance are engineering concerns, not afterthoughts
  • Control, evaluation and efficiency remain open problems
