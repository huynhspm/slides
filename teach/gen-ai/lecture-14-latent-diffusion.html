1.
Why Latent Space

The Cost of Pixel Space

  • A \( 512\times512\times3 \) image is 786,432 dimensions
  • Every one of 50+ denoising steps runs at that resolution
  • Training such a model costs hundreds of GPU-days

Most Pixels Are Not Semantic

  • Most bits encode imperceptible high-frequency detail
  • Diffusion wastes capacity modelling texture noise
  • Separate perceptual compression from semantic generation

Latent Diffusion

Run the entire diffusion process inside a compressed latent space learned by an autoencoder.
  • Encode once: \( z = \mathcal{E}(x) \)
  • Diffuse and denoise in \( z \)
  • Decode once: \( \hat{x} = \mathcal{D}(z) \)

The Compression Factor

  • \( 512\times512\times3 \rightarrow 64\times64\times4 \) is a 48× reduction
  • Training and sampling cost fall by a similar factor
  • This is what put text-to-image on consumer GPUs

The Autoencoder Matters

  • Trained with perceptual and adversarial losses (Lecture 07)
  • Mild KL regularisation keeps the latent well-scaled
  • If the decoder cannot render it, no diffusion model can generate it

2.
The U-Net

The U-Net Backbone

  • Downsampling encoder, bottleneck, upsampling decoder
  • Skip connections carry detail across the bottleneck
  • Residual blocks at each resolution

Time Conditioning

$$ \text{emb}(t) = \big[\sin(\omega_k t), \cos(\omega_k t)\big]_{k=1}^{K} $$
  • One network serves all timesteps
  • A sinusoidal embedding is projected into every residual block
  • The network learns how much noise to expect at each \( t \)

Attention in the U-Net

  • Self-attention at the lower resolutions
  • Convolutions alone cannot relate distant regions
  • This is also where the text conditioning will enter

3.
Text Conditioning

Encoding the Prompt

  • A frozen text encoder maps the prompt to a sequence of embeddings
  • CLIP or T5 — pretrained, not learned with the diffusion model
  • Text quality bounds image quality

Cross-Attention

$$ \text{Attention}(Q, K, V), \qquad Q = W_Q \varphi(z_t), \quad K = W_K \tau(c), \quad V = W_V \tau(c) $$
  • Queries come from the image latent
  • Keys and values come from the text embeddings
  • Every spatial position asks: which words apply to me?

Where Conditioning Enters

  • Cross-attention layers sit inside every U-Net block
  • The condition therefore influences every resolution
  • Combined with classifier-free guidance from Lecture 13

Stable Diffusion, Assembled

  • VAE — pixels ↔ latent
  • U-Net — denoising inside the latent
  • Text encoder — prompt to embeddings
  • Cross-attention — the link between the two
  • Scheduler — the sampling trajectory

4.
Control

Image-to-Image

  • Encode the source image, then add noise only partway
  • Denoise from there with the new prompt
  • The noise level controls how much of the original survives

Inpainting

$$ x_{t-1} \leftarrow m \odot x_{t-1}^{\text{known}} + (1-m) \odot x_{t-1}^{\text{generated}} $$
  • At each step, overwrite the known region with the true noisy image
  • Only the masked region is genuinely generated
  • Context flows in through attention, so the fill is coherent

The Limits of Prompting

  • Text cannot specify exact pose, layout or geometry
  • "A person sitting" cannot express which pose
  • We need spatial conditioning

ControlNet

  • Clone the U-Net encoder as a trainable copy; freeze the original
  • Connect them with zero-initialised convolutions
  • Training starts as an exact no-op and adds control gradually
  • Conditions: edges, depth, pose, segmentation, scribbles
[Figure: ControlNet Conditioning]
Description: One spatial condition driving several generated images.
Image prompt: A pose skeleton on the left, and three generated images on the right that all match that exact pose in different styles.

Why Zero-Initialisation Matters

  • The frozen base model is never damaged
  • No harmful gradients at the start of training
  • Control can be trained on relatively small datasets

5.
The Complete Picture

The Full Stack

  • Lecture 07 gave the autoencoder
  • Lecture 12 gave the denoising objective
  • Lecture 13 gave guidance and fast sampling
  • Today assembles them into a controllable system

Remaining Weaknesses

  • Compositional prompts and counting still fail
  • Text rendering inside images is unreliable
  • The autoencoder caps the achievable detail
  • Still 20–50 network calls per image

Summary

  • Latent diffusion moves the process into a compressed space
  • The U-Net denoises, conditioned on time
  • Cross-attention injects text at every resolution
  • Inpainting and image-to-image are sampling-time modifications
  • ControlNet adds spatial control without retraining the base model
