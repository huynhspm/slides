1.
Encoder and Decoder

From Lecture 03 to a Model

  • Lecture 03 gave us the ELBO as a general inference tool
  • A VAE is what happens when both distributions are neural networks
  • Same bound, now with an encoder and a decoder

The Probabilistic Encoder

$$ q_\phi(z \mid x) = \mathcal{N}\big(z; \mu_\phi(x), \operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_\phi(x))\big) $$
  • A network outputs a distribution, not a point
  • \( \mu_\phi(x) \) and \( \log \sigma^2_\phi(x) \) are its two heads
  • Diagonal covariance keeps it cheap

The Probabilistic Decoder

$$ p_\theta(x \mid z) $$
  • Gaussian for continuous data → an \( \ell_2 \) reconstruction term
  • Bernoulli for binary data → a cross-entropy term
  • The choice of decoder is the choice of reconstruction loss

The Architecture

  • \( x \rightarrow \) encoder \( \rightarrow (\mu, \sigma) \rightarrow z \rightarrow \) decoder \( \rightarrow \hat{x} \)
  • Looks like an autoencoder, but the bottleneck is stochastic
  • That single change is what makes it generative

2.
The VAE Objective

The Objective

$$ \mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi;x) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)] - D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q_\phi(z \mid x) \,\|\, p(z)\big) $$
  • Maximise the ELBO over both \( \theta \) and \( \phi \)
  • Term 1 rebuilds the input; term 2 shapes the latent space

The KL Has a Closed Form

$$ D_{\mathrm{KL}} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left(\mu_j^2 + \sigma_j^2 - \log \sigma_j^2 - 1\right) $$
  • For a Gaussian posterior and \( \mathcal{N}(0,I) \) prior
  • No sampling needed for this term — it is computed exactly
  • Only the reconstruction term needs Monte Carlo

The Sampling Problem

We must backpropagate through \( z \sim q_\phi(z \mid x) \) — but sampling is not differentiable.
  • The gradient path to the encoder is broken
  • Score-function estimators work but have very high variance

The Reparameterization Trick

$$ z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) $$
  • Move the randomness into a parameter-free variable \( \epsilon \)
  • \( z \) is now a deterministic function of \( \mu, \sigma, \epsilon \)
  • Gradients flow to the encoder; variance is low

3.
Training and Generation

The Training Loop

  • Encode \( x \) to \( \mu, \sigma \)
  • Sample \( \epsilon \), form \( z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon \)
  • Decode \( z \), compute reconstruction + KL
  • Backpropagate through everything, end to end

Generation

$$ z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \qquad x \sim p_\theta(x \mid z) $$
  • At generation time the encoder is discarded
  • One forward pass — far faster than autoregressive sampling
  • The prior must match the aggregate posterior for this to work

Latent Interpolation

  • Interpolate \( z_\alpha = (1-\alpha)z_1 + \alpha z_2 \) and decode
  • Smooth semantic transitions indicate a well-structured latent space
  • The KL term is what makes the space continuous enough for this

4.
Strengths and Limitations

Why VAE Samples Look Blurry

  • A Gaussian decoder makes the loss an average over plausible outputs
  • Averaging sharp alternatives produces a blurry mean
  • Also: the reverse-KL direction from Lecture 02 is at work

Strengths

  • Stable training — a single well-behaved objective
  • A meaningful, continuous latent space
  • Fast one-step sampling
  • Principled probabilistic formulation

Limitations

  • Blurry samples compared with GANs or diffusion
  • Only a lower bound on the likelihood, not the value
  • Posterior collapse when the decoder is too strong

Summary

  • A VAE instantiates the ELBO with a neural encoder and decoder
  • The encoder outputs a distribution \( q_\phi(z\mid x) \)
  • The reparameterization trick makes sampling differentiable
  • The KL term has a closed form for Gaussian posterior and prior
  • Stable and fast, but samples are blurry — Lecture 07 addresses this
