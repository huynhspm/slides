1.
Scale

From Single Models to Foundations

  • Early models were trained per dataset: faces, bedrooms, digits
  • Modern models train once on web-scale image-text data
  • One model then serves an open-ended range of prompts

Image-Text Pairs

  • Datasets of hundreds of millions to billions of pairs
  • Alt-text supervision is free but noisy
  • Filtering and recaptioning matter as much as model size

Data Quality Over Data Quantity

  • Aesthetic and resolution filtering
  • Synthetic recaptioning improves prompt following markedly
  • Deduplication reduces memorisation
  • Often the highest-leverage change available

Scaling Behaviour

  • Loss improves predictably with compute, data and parameters
  • Some capabilities appear only past a scale threshold
  • Predictable scaling is what justifies the investment

2.
Vision-Language Representations

Vision-Language Representations

Generation needs text and images to live in a shared embedding space.
  • The text encoder must understand what the prompt describes
  • That understanding is learned separately, at scale

CLIP

$$ \mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \frac{\exp\big(\cos(I_i, T_i)/\tau\big)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} \exp\big(\cos(I_i, T_j)/\tau\big)} $$
  • Contrastive learning over a batch of pairs
  • Matched image-text pairs are pulled together, all others pushed apart
  • The batch supplies the negatives — larger batches help
[Figure: Contrastive Image-Text Learning]
Description: A similarity matrix with matched pairs on the diagonal.
Image prompt: An N-by-N grid of image rows and text columns, diagonal cells highlighted as positives and off-diagonal cells shaded as negatives.

Why CLIP Mattered

  • Zero-shot classification without task-specific training
  • A text encoder usable by any conditional generator
  • And an alignment metric, as seen in Lecture 16

3.
From U-Net to Transformer

The Limits of the U-Net

  • Convolutional inductive bias is helpful but restrictive
  • Scaling behaviour is less predictable than for Transformers
  • The rest of deep learning had already converged on one architecture

Patchification

  • Split the latent into non-overlapping patches
  • Flatten each patch into a token
  • The image becomes a sequence, as in Lecture 05

Diffusion Transformers

  • Replace the U-Net entirely with a Transformer over latent patches
  • Timestep and class conditioning via adaptive layer norm
  • Text conditioning via cross-attention, as before
[Figure: U-Net vs DiT]
Description: The two denoiser architectures side by side.
Image prompt: Left: U-Net with downsampling, bottleneck, upsampling and skip connections. Right: patchified latent into a stack of identical Transformer blocks.

Scaling Behaviour of DiT

  • Sample quality improves smoothly with model FLOPs
  • No architectural redesign needed to grow the model
  • This predictability is why recent systems adopted it

The Convergence

  • Language, vision and generation now share one backbone
  • The Transformer plus a tokenizer or patchifier
  • Only the data and the objective differ

4.
Beyond Images

Video Generation

  • Add a time axis: the model must be temporally consistent
  • Spatio-temporal attention, or factorised spatial and temporal blocks
  • Cost: memory grows with frame count
  • Overview only — this is a course on image generation

3D Generation

  • Represented as NeRFs, meshes or Gaussian splats
  • Very little 3D training data compared with images
  • Score distillation lifts a 2D image model into 3D

Audio and Multimodal

  • Audio generated as spectrograms or waveform tokens
  • The same diffusion and autoregressive recipes transfer
  • Unified models increasingly generate several modalities at once

Summary

  • Modern systems train once on web-scale image-text data
  • Data quality often matters more than raw quantity
  • CLIP provides the shared text-image space that conditioning needs
  • DiT replaces the U-Net and scales predictably
  • Video, 3D and audio reuse the same recipes at greater cost
