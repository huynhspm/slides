1.
The Score Function

The Score Function

$$ s(x) = \nabla_x \log p(x) $$
  • A vector field pointing toward higher density
  • From Lecture 11: it does not depend on the normalising constant
  • So we can learn it without ever knowing \( p(x) \)

Score Matching

$$ \mathcal{J}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}\big[\| s_\theta(x) - \nabla_x \log p_{\text{data}}(x) \|^2 \big] $$
  • The target is unknown, so this cannot be used directly
  • Integration by parts removes it, but leaves a costly trace term

Denoising Score Matching

$$ \mathbb{E}\big[\| s_\theta(\tilde{x}) - \nabla_{\tilde{x}} \log q(\tilde{x} \mid x) \|^2\big], \qquad \tilde{x} = x + \sigma\epsilon $$
  • Perturb the data, then match the score of the known noise kernel
  • For a Gaussian kernel the target is simply \( -\epsilon/\sigma \)
  • Tractable, and it needs no knowledge of \( p_{\text{data}} \)

Denoising Is Score Estimation

$$ s_\theta(x_t, t) = -\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} $$
  • The DDPM noise predictor is a scaled score model
  • Two literatures, two derivations, one network
  • This identity unifies Lectures 11, 12 and 13

2.
Continuous Time

Taking the Limit

  • DDPM used \( T = 1000 \) discrete steps
  • Let the step size go to zero: the chain becomes a continuous process
  • Discrete diffusion is one particular discretisation

The Forward SDE

$$ dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw $$
  • \( f \) is the drift, \( g \) the diffusion coefficient, \( w \) a Wiener process
  • VP-SDE corresponds to DDPM; VE-SDE to score matching
  • One framework covering both prior lectures

The Reverse-Time SDE

$$ dx = \big[f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big] dt + g(t)\, d\bar{w} $$
  • Anderson's theorem: the reverse of a diffusion is also a diffusion
  • It requires exactly one unknown — the score
  • Which is what we trained the network to provide

The Probability-Flow ODE

$$ \frac{dx}{dt} = f(x,t) - \frac{1}{2}g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x) $$
  • A deterministic path with the same marginals as the SDE
  • Enables fast black-box ODE solvers
  • And gives an exact likelihood via the continuous change of variables

3.
Fast Sampling

Why Sampling Is Slow

  • The ancestral sampler follows the Markov chain step by step
  • Small steps are needed to keep the Gaussian assumption valid
  • Hundreds of network calls per image

DDIM

$$ x_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\, \hat{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2}\quad \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t) + \sigma_t z $$
  • A non-Markovian process with the same training objective
  • \( \sigma_t = 0 \) makes sampling fully deterministic
  • Skip timesteps: 1000 steps become 50 with little quality loss

Deterministic Sampling Is Useful

  • The same seed always gives the same image
  • The latent becomes invertible, enabling real-image editing
  • Semantic interpolation in noise space becomes meaningful

Faster Solvers

  • The probability-flow ODE lets us borrow numerical analysis
  • DPM-Solver and friends reach good samples in 10–20 steps
  • The ceiling is the ODE's curvature — which Lecture 15 attacks

4.
Guidance

Conditional Generation

$$ \nabla_x \log p(x \mid c) = \nabla_x \log p(x) + \nabla_x \log p(c \mid x) $$
  • By Bayes rule, the conditional score splits in two
  • An unconditional score plus a correction toward the condition

Classifier Guidance

$$ \hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t) - w\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\, \nabla_x \log p_\phi(c \mid x_t) $$
  • Train a separate classifier on noisy images
  • Its gradient steers sampling toward the target class
  • Drawback: a second model, trained on noisy data

Classifier-Free Guidance

$$ \hat{\epsilon} = (1+w)\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,c) - w\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,\varnothing) $$
  • Train one model, randomly dropping the condition
  • At sampling time, extrapolate away from the unconditional prediction
  • No classifier needed — this is what every modern system uses

The Guidance Trade-off

  • Higher \( w \) → stronger prompt adherence, higher fidelity
  • But lower diversity and eventually oversaturation
  • The quality-versus-diversity trade-off from Lecture 01, once again

Summary

  • The score \( \nabla_x \log p(x) \) avoids the normalising constant
  • Denoising score matching makes it learnable, and equals \( \epsilon \)-prediction
  • The SDE view unifies DDPM and score matching in continuous time
  • The probability-flow ODE gives fast deterministic sampling and exact likelihood
  • Classifier-free guidance trades diversity for prompt adherence
