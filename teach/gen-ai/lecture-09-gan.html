1.
Implicit Generation

Dropping the Likelihood

What if we never write down \( p_\theta(x) \) at all, and only learn to sample?
  • Every model so far needed a tractable density or a bound
  • That requirement constrains the architecture
  • An implicit model keeps only the sampler

The Generator

$$ z \sim p(z), \qquad x = G_\theta(z) $$
  • An unrestricted neural network — no invertibility, no factorization
  • It defines a distribution we can sample but cannot evaluate
  • So we need a training signal that does not require a density

The Discriminator

  • A classifier \( D_\psi(x) \in [0,1] \): real or generated?
  • It supplies the learning signal for the generator
  • The generator wins by making the discriminator fail

The Adversarial Game

$$ \min_G \max_D \quad \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{p(z)}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))] $$
  • \( D \) maximises: separate real from fake
  • \( G \) minimises: fool the discriminator
  • A two-player minimax game, not a single loss

2.
The Theory

The Optimal Discriminator

$$ D^\star(x) = \frac{p_{\text{data}}(x)}{p_{\text{data}}(x) + p_g(x)} $$
  • Obtained by maximising the objective pointwise in \( D \)
  • It equals \( 1/2 \) everywhere exactly when \( p_g = p_{\text{data}} \)

The Implied Divergence

$$ \text{With } D^\star \text{ fixed:} \quad \min_G \quad 2\,D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p_{\text{data}} \,\|\, p_g) - \log 4 $$
  • GAN training minimises the Jensen–Shannon divergence
  • Symmetric, unlike the KL of maximum likelihood
  • The global optimum is \( p_g = p_{\text{data}} \)

Why the Theory Is Not Enough

  • The result assumes \( D \) is trained to optimality at every step
  • In practice both networks take alternating small steps
  • JS divergence saturates when the two distributions barely overlap

The Non-Saturating Loss

$$ \max_G \quad \mathbb{E}_{p(z)}[\log D(G(z))] $$
  • The original generator loss has vanishing gradients early on
  • This variant flips the objective to keep gradients strong
  • Used in essentially every practical implementation

3.
DCGAN and Latent Structure

DCGAN

  • Strided and transposed convolutions instead of pooling
  • Batch normalisation in both networks
  • ReLU in the generator, LeakyReLU in the discriminator
  • The first architecture recipe that trained reliably

The Latent Space

  • Interpolating \( z \) gives smooth semantic transitions
  • Vector arithmetic works: man with glasses − man + woman
  • Structure emerges without ever being supervised

Training Dynamics

  • The two losses are not a joint objective being minimised
  • A falling generator loss does not mean better samples
  • Equilibrium, not convergence, is what we want

4.
Failure Modes

Mode Collapse

The generator maps many latents to the same few outputs — high quality, no diversity.
  • Fooling \( D \) with one perfect sample is a valid local strategy
  • Nothing in the objective explicitly rewards coverage
[Figure: Mode Collapse]
Description: A generator covering only some modes of a multimodal target.
Image prompt: Two panels over an eight-mode ring of Gaussians: left shows samples on all eight modes, right shows samples clustered on only two.

Training Instability

  • Oscillation: the two networks chase each other endlessly
  • Vanishing gradients when the discriminator gets too strong
  • Sensitive to learning rates and architecture choices

Why GANs Work at All

  • The discriminator provides a learned loss function
  • It focuses on whatever currently gives the fake away
  • That is a far better perceptual signal than pixel-wise \( \ell_2 \)

Summary

  • GANs are implicit: they sample without a density
  • Training is a two-player minimax game, not one loss
  • The optimal discriminator implies minimising JS divergence
  • A learned loss gives sharp samples that likelihood methods miss
  • Mode collapse and instability are the price
