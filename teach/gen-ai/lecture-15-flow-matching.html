1.
Continuous Flows

Continuous Flows

$$ \frac{dx_t}{dt} = v_\theta(x_t, t) $$
  • One vector field replaces a stack of layers
  • Integrating it carries noise to data; invertibility is free

The Old Problem

  • Training a CNF by maximum likelihood needs backpropagation through an ODE solver
  • Expensive, memory-hungry and unstable
  • This is why CNFs lost to diffusion for years

Probability Paths

$$ p_0 = \mathcal{N}(0, I) \quad \longrightarrow \quad p_1 = p_{\text{data}} $$
  • A path of distributions interpolating noise and data
  • Every generative model in Part 4 defines such a path
  • Diffusion picks one particular path; we can pick others

The Key Insight

If we choose the probability path ourselves, its generating vector field is known in closed form — so we can regress onto it directly.
  • No ODE solving during training
  • No adversarial game, no variational bound

2.
The Flow Matching Objective

The Flow Matching Objective

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, p_t(x)} \big\| v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x) \big\|^2 $$
  • Regress the network onto the target vector field \( u_t \)
  • Simple mean-squared error, like diffusion
  • But: the marginal \( u_t \) is still intractable

Conditional Flow Matching

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_1, p_t(x \mid x_1)} \big\| v_\theta(x,t) - u_t(x \mid x_1) \big\|^2 $$
  • Condition on a single data point \( x_1 \) — now everything is closed form
  • The two objectives have the same gradients
  • The intractable problem becomes a trivial one

The Simplest Path

$$ x_t = (1-t)\, x_0 + t\, x_1, \qquad u_t(x \mid x_1) = x_1 - x_0 $$
  • A straight line between noise and data
  • The target velocity is constant — just the difference vector
  • Training reduces to predicting \( x_1 - x_0 \)

Training in Four Lines

  • Sample \( x_1 \) from data and \( x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) \)
  • Sample \( t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1] \), form \( x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1 \)
  • Predict \( v_\theta(x_t, t) \)
  • Regress it onto \( x_1 - x_0 \)

3.
Rectified Flow

Why Straightness Matters

  • Sampling means numerically integrating the ODE
  • Step count is driven by trajectory curvature
  • A perfectly straight path is exact in one Euler step

Rectified Flow

  • Train a flow, then generate \( (x_0, x_1) \) pairs by sampling it
  • Retrain on those coupled pairs
  • Each such reflow round straightens the trajectories further

Optimal Transport Paths

  • Independent noise-data pairs give crossing trajectories
  • Minibatch OT coupling pairs them more sensibly
  • Straighter paths from the very first training run

Few-Step Generation

  • Diffusion needs 20–50 steps; rectified flows reach good quality in 1–4
  • Sampling cost falls by an order of magnitude
  • This is why recent large models adopted the formulation

4.
Unifying the Picture

Diffusion Is a Flow

  • The probability-flow ODE of Lecture 13 is a continuous flow
  • Diffusion corresponds to one specific, curved probability path
  • Flow matching simply lets us choose a better one

Comparing the Two

  • Objective: predict noise vs predict velocity
  • Path: fixed by the noise schedule vs freely chosen
  • Sampling: SDE or ODE vs ODE
  • Steps: tens vs a handful

A Single Family

Score matching, diffusion and flow matching are three views of learning to transport noise into data.
  • The differences are the path and the parameterization
  • Each new framing bought a practical improvement

Summary

  • Flow matching regresses a vector field onto a chosen probability path
  • Conditional flow matching makes the target tractable with identical gradients
  • Linear paths give a constant target velocity — training is trivial
  • Rectified flow straightens trajectories for few-step sampling
  • Diffusion is the special case where the path is dictated by the noise schedule
