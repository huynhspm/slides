1.
Latent Variable Models

Why Latent Variables?

Real data has hidden structure: pose, style, identity, lighting. None of it is labelled.
  • A flat density over pixels ignores this structure
  • Latent variables give the model a place to put it

The Latent-Variable Formulation

$$ p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x \mid z)\, p(z)\, dz $$
  • \( z \) — an unobserved latent variable
  • \( p(z) \) — a simple prior, usually \( \mathcal{N}(0, I) \)
  • \( p_\theta(x \mid z) \) — the decoder or likelihood

Prior, Likelihood, Posterior

  • Prior \( p(z) \) — what we assume before seeing data
  • Likelihood \( p_\theta(x \mid z) \) — how a latent produces data
  • Posterior \( p_\theta(z \mid x) \) — which latent explains this \( x \)

Generation Is Easy

$$ z \sim p(z), \qquad x \sim p_\theta(x \mid z) $$
  • Ancestral sampling: draw a latent, then decode it
  • Sampling is cheap — the difficulty is training

2.
Gaussian Mixtures & EM

Gaussian Mixture Models

$$ p(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \pi_k \, \mathcal{N}(x; \mu_k, \Sigma_k) $$
  • The latent \( z \in \{1,\ldots,K\} \) selects which component generated \( x \)
  • The simplest useful latent-variable model

The Responsibility

$$ \gamma_{ik} = p(z_i = k \mid x_i) = \frac{\pi_k \mathcal{N}(x_i; \mu_k, \Sigma_k)}{\sum_j \pi_j \mathcal{N}(x_i; \mu_j, \Sigma_j)} $$
  • The posterior over the latent, and here it is tractable
  • A soft assignment of each point to each component

The EM Algorithm

  • E-step: compute the posterior \( \gamma_{ik} \) with parameters fixed
  • M-step: update \( \pi_k, \mu_k, \Sigma_k \) with the posterior fixed
  • Each iteration never decreases the likelihood

Why GMMs Are Not Enough

  • Needs \( K \) components to cover \( K \) modes — hopeless in pixel space
  • Assumes a fixed simple form per component
  • But it makes the posterior tractable, which real models will not

3.
The Intractable Posterior

The Intractable Posterior

$$ p_\theta(z \mid x) = \frac{p_\theta(x \mid z)p(z)}{p_\theta(x)}, \qquad p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x \mid z)p(z)\,dz $$
  • With a neural decoder that integral has no closed form
  • High dimension makes numerical integration hopeless
  • So the posterior is unavailable — and so is the likelihood

The Variational Idea

Approximate the true posterior with a simpler distribution \( q_\phi(z \mid x) \) we can compute.
  • Turn inference into optimisation
  • Choose \( q_\phi \) from a tractable family, e.g. a Gaussian
  • Fit \( \phi \) so \( q_\phi(z\mid x) \approx p_\theta(z \mid x) \)

Amortized Inference

  • Naively, each \( x_i \) would need its own optimisation
  • Instead train one encoder network mapping \( x \mapsto q_\phi(z \mid x) \)
  • The cost of inference is amortized across the dataset

4.
Variational Inference & the ELBO

Deriving a Lower Bound

$$ \log p_\theta(x) = \log \int q_\phi(z\mid x)\, \frac{p_\theta(x, z)}{q_\phi(z \mid x)}\, dz $$
  • Multiply and divide by \( q_\phi(z\mid x) \) — an expectation appears
  • Now apply Jensen's inequality from Lecture 02

The ELBO

$$ \log p_\theta(x) \quad \ge \quad \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z \mid x)}\!\left[\log \frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q_\phi(z \mid x)}\right] = \mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi;x) $$
  • The Evidence Lower BOund
  • Maximising it pushes up a bound on the true log-likelihood

Two Readings of the ELBO

$$ \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)]}_{\text{reconstruction}} - \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(z\mid x) \,\|\, p(z))}_{\text{regularisation}} $$
  • Reconstruction: the decoder should rebuild \( x \) from \( z \)
  • Regularisation: the posterior should stay near the prior

The Gap Is Exactly a KL

$$ \log p_\theta(x) - \mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi;x) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x) \,\|\, p_\theta(z \mid x)\big) $$
  • The bound is tight exactly when \( q_\phi \) matches the true posterior
  • Maximising the ELBO does two jobs at once: fits the model and improves inference

The Reconstruction–Regularisation Trade-off

  • Push reconstruction alone → the latent space becomes a lookup table
  • Push the KL alone → the posterior collapses to the prior and ignores \( x \)
  • Good generative behaviour lives between the two

Summary

  • Latent variables model hidden structure: \( p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x\mid z)p(z)dz \)
  • GMM + EM works because its posterior is tractable
  • With neural decoders the posterior is intractable
  • Variational inference replaces it with a learned \( q_\phi(z \mid x) \)
  • The ELBO = reconstruction − KL, and its gap is a KL to the true posterior
