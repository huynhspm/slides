1.
The Autoregressive Factorization

The Core Idea

$$ p(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{D} p(x_i \mid x_{<i}) $$
  • No approximation — this is the chain rule, exactly
  • One hard density becomes \( D \) small predictions
  • Each factor can be a neural network

Ordering the Pixels

  • The chain rule needs a fixed order
  • Images have no natural order — we impose one (usually raster scan)
  • Different orderings give different models of the same data
[Figure: Raster-Scan Ordering]

Image prompt: An 8x8 pixel grid numbered in raster order; pixels before the current one shaded blue, current pixel highlighted.

Training: Teacher Forcing

  • At training time the true prefix \( x_{<i} \) is always fed in
  • All \( D \) conditionals are evaluated in parallel
  • Loss is the summed negative log-likelihood

The Training Objective

$$ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{i=1}^{D} \log p_\theta(x_i \mid x_{<i}) $$
  • Exactly maximum likelihood — no bound, no approximation
  • This is why autoregressive models report clean likelihood numbers

2.
Neural Autoregressive Models

NADE

  • Models each conditional with a shared hidden representation
  • Weight sharing keeps the parameter count linear in \( D \)
  • The first practical neural autoregressive density estimator

PixelRNN

  • An LSTM reads pixels in sequence
  • Unbounded context: every previous pixel can influence the next
  • Cost: inherently sequential, so training is slow

PixelCNN

  • Replace recurrence with a convolution
  • Training becomes fully parallel across pixels
  • Requirement: the convolution must not look ahead

Masked Convolutions

Zero out the kernel weights covering the current pixel and everything after it.
  • Mask A — excludes the centre pixel (first layer)
  • Mask B — includes the centre pixel (later layers)
  • Preserves the autoregressive property layer after layer
[Figure: Masked Convolution Kernel]
Description: A 5x5 kernel with the causal half enabled and the rest zeroed.
Image prompt: A 5x5 grid: upper rows and left of centre filled blue, centre marked, lower-right cells greyed to zero.

The Blind Spot

  • Naive masking leaves a region of context unreachable
  • Fixed by splitting into vertical and horizontal stacks
  • Gated PixelCNN adds gated activations and conditioning

3.
Sampling

Sampling Is Sequential

$$ x_1 \sim p(x_1), \quad x_2 \sim p(x_2 \mid x_1), \quad \ldots, \quad x_D \sim p(x_D \mid x_{<D}) $$
  • Each pixel needs a full forward pass
  • A \( 256 \times 256 \) image needs 65,536 passes
  • This is the defining weakness of the family

Train Fast, Sample Slow

  • Training: parallel — all conditionals at once
  • Sampling: strictly sequential — one step at a time
  • The asymmetry comes directly from the factorization
[Figure: Parallel Training vs Sequential Sampling]
Description: Training evaluates all positions simultaneously; sampling walks one position at a time.
Image prompt: Two rows: top shows all pixels computed in one parallel block; bottom shows a chain of arrows generating pixels one by one.

Exposure Bias

  • Training always conditions on real prefixes
  • Sampling conditions on the model's own outputs
  • Small errors early can compound over a long sequence

4.
Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Exact likelihood — no bound, directly comparable
  • Stable maximum-likelihood training, no adversarial dynamics
  • Naturally handles discrete data

Limitations

  • Slow sampling — \( O(D) \) sequential steps
  • Requires an arbitrary ordering of a 2D signal
  • No compact latent space for editing or interpolation

Where This Goes Next

  • Lecture 05 replaces the CNN with a Transformer
  • And replaces pixels with a much shorter sequence of visual tokens
  • The same chain rule, applied at a smarter granularity

Summary

  • Autoregressive models factorize \( p(x) = \prod_i p(x_i \mid x_{<i}) \) exactly
  • They require a fixed generation order over the pixels
  • Masked convolutions keep the model causal while training in parallel
  • Training is parallel but sampling is inherently sequential
  • Exact likelihood is the reward; slow sampling is the price
