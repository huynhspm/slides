1.
What is a Feature?

From Pixels to Features

Feature: A piece of meaningful, distinctive information extracted from an image — an edge, corner, blob, or region — more compact than raw pixels.

  • This is mid-level vision: image → features / regions of interest
  • Good features are repeatable, distinctive, and local
  • They power matching, recognition, tracking, and reconstruction

Types of Features

Edges

Boundaries where intensity changes sharply.

Corners

Points where two edges meet — highly localizable.

Blobs

Regions that differ from their surroundings.

Lines & shapes

Geometric primitives spanning the image.

Key Idea: Different tasks need different features — but nearly all start from local intensity change.

2.
Edge Detection

What is an Edge?

An edge is a significant local change in intensity — usually the boundary of an object.

  • The first derivative peaks at an edge
  • The second derivative has a zero-crossing at an edge

The Image Gradient

The gradient points toward the steepest intensity increase:

$$\nabla f = \Big[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\Big], \quad |\nabla f| = \sqrt{f_x^2 + f_y^2}, \quad \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\frac{f_y}{f_x}$$

  • Approximate $f_x, f_y$ with Sobel / Prewitt kernels
  • Magnitude = edge strength; angle = edge direction
  • Threshold the magnitude to get an edge map

LoG & Second-Order Detection

The Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) smooths first, then takes the second derivative:

$$\text{LoG} = \nabla^2\big(G_\sigma * f\big)$$

  • Gaussian blur suppresses noise before differentiation
  • Edges = zero-crossings of the LoG response
  • Also called the "Mexican hat" filter

The Canny Edge Detector

1

Smooth with a Gaussian to reduce noise

2

Gradient — compute magnitude & direction

3

Non-maximum suppression — thin edges to 1 pixel

4

Hysteresis thresholding — link strong & weak edges

Canny remains the classic, high-quality edge detector.

3.
Corners & Blobs

Why Corners?

Edges are ambiguous along their direction (the aperture problem). Corners are localizable in both directions.

  • Flat region: no change in any direction
  • Edge: change in one direction
  • Corner: change in all directions
[Figure placeholder]

Harris Corner Detector

Analyze the second-moment matrix of gradients in a window:

$$M = \sum_{w} \begin{bmatrix} f_x^2 & f_x f_y \\ f_x f_y & f_y^2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad R = \det(M) - k\,[\text{tr}(M)]^2$$

  • Both eigenvalues large → corner
  • One large → edge; both small → flat
  • $R$ is large & positive at corners; invariant to rotation

Blob Detection

A blob is a region brighter or darker than its surroundings, detected at a characteristic scale.

  • Apply LoG (or its fast approximation DoG) at multiple scales
  • Blobs = extrema in space and scale
  • The basis of scale-invariant detectors (SIFT)

4.
Line Detection

The Hough Transform

A voting technique that finds lines even when they are broken or noisy.

$$\rho = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$$

  • Each edge point votes for all lines $(\rho, \theta)$ passing through it
  • Votes accumulate in a $(\rho, \theta)$ accumulator array
  • Peaks in the accumulator = detected lines

Hough in Practice

Typical pipeline:

  1. Detect edges (e.g. Canny)
  2. Each edge pixel votes in $(\rho, \theta)$ space
  3. Find accumulator peaks
  4. Map peaks back to image lines

Extends to circles and other parametric shapes.

Summary

  • Features are compact, distinctive structures extracted from pixels — the mid-level of vision.
  • Edges: gradient magnitude, LoG zero-crossings, and the multi-step Canny detector.
  • Corners (Harris) and blobs (LoG/DoG) are localizable interest points.
  • The Hough transform detects lines and shapes by voting.
