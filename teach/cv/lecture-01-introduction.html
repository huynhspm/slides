What is Image Processing?

Definition: Image processing covers the techniques for acquiring, representing, storing, and manipulating images to produce a desired result.

Acquire

Camera, scanner, X-ray, satellite, MRI…

Represent

Grayscale, color, video, size, resolution

Store

Matrices, file formats, compression

Manipulate

Edit, find objects, classify, understand

Three Levels of Vision

LOW
Low-level — Image Processing

Image $\rightarrow$ Image. Modifies pixel values (denoise, enhance, filter). Part 2.

MID
Mid-level — Image Representation

Image $\rightarrow$ Features / regions. Extracts edges, keypoints, segments. Part 3.

HIGH
High-level — Image Understanding

Image $\rightarrow$ Description / decisions. Recognizes objects and scenes. Part 4.

Key Idea: As we move from low to high level, the output becomes more abstract — from raw pixels toward meaning.

Image Processing vs. Computer Vision

Image Processing Computer Vision
Image in $\rightarrow$ image out Image in $\rightarrow$ understanding out
Focus on signal / pixel manipulation Focus on interpretation & semantics
Mostly low & mid level Spans low, mid & high level

The boundary is increasingly blurred in the deep learning era.

History & Future

A Brief History

1920s

First digital image application — the Bartlane system transmits coded newspaper pictures between London and New York by cable.

1964

Computers enhance images of the moon from Ranger 7 — the space race ignites digital image processing.

1970s

Medical imaging takes off — CT (tomography) earns the 1979 Nobel Prize in Medicine.

1980s+

Vision spreads into nearly every field — restoration, art, medicine, industry, law, human–computer interaction.

2010s+

The deep learning era — CNNs and transformers redefine what machines can see.

The Future

Deeper learning

Vision increasingly powered by deep neural networks trained on massive data.

Blurred boundaries

The line between image processing and computer vision continues to fade.

Key Idea: Classical techniques still matter — they are the foundation on which modern deep vision is built.

Summary

  • Image processing acquires, represents, stores, and manipulates images; computer vision aims at understanding them.
  • Vision spans three levels: low (pixels), mid (features), and high (meaning).
  • The field evolved from 1920s image transmission to today's deep learning era.
  • Applications reach medicine, autonomy, industry, remote sensing, and more.
  • This course builds from foundations up — classical techniques remain essential.
