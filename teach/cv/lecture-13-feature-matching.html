1.
Template Matching

The Matching Problem

Goal: Find where a template appears in an image — or find which features in two images correspond to the same point.

  • Slide the template over the image and score the similarity at each location
  • The best-scoring position is the match
  • The choice of similarity measure is crucial

SSD & SAD

SSD — Sum of Squared Differences

$$\sum_{x,y}\big(I(x,y) - T(x,y)\big)^2$$

SAD — Sum of Absolute Differences

$$\sum_{x,y}\big|I(x,y) - T(x,y)\big|$$

Key Idea: Smaller = better match. Simple and fast, but sensitive to brightness changes.

Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC)

Subtract the mean and normalize — robust to brightness and contrast changes:

$$\text{NCC} = \frac{\sum (I - \bar{I})(T - \bar{T})} {\sqrt{\sum (I - \bar{I})^2 \sum (T - \bar{T})^2}}$$

  • Ranges from $-1$ to $+1$; $+1$ is a perfect match
  • Geometrically, the dot product of unit vectors
  • Invariant to linear intensity changes — the practical default

2.
Descriptor Matching

Matching Descriptor Vectors

Match features by the distance between their descriptor vectors:

Euclidean ($L_2$)

For real-valued descriptors (SIFT, HOG)

Hamming

For binary descriptors (ORB, BRIEF)

The closest descriptor is the nearest neighbor — but not every nearest neighbor is a true match.

Lowe's Ratio Test

Compare the nearest and second-nearest neighbor distances:

$$\frac{d_1}{d_2} < 0.8 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{accept}$$

A good match is much closer than the next best — ambiguous matches are rejected.

3.
Robust Estimation

Outliers & Geometry

  • Even after the ratio test, some matches are wrong (outliers)
  • True matches must obey a consistent geometric transform (e.g. a homography)
  • We need to fit that transform while ignoring outliers

Key Idea: Geometric consistency separates real matches from random ones.

RANSAC

1

Sample a minimal set of matches at random

2

Fit a candidate model (e.g. homography)

3

Count inliers that agree with the model

4

Repeat and keep the model with the most inliers

RANdom SAmple Consensus — robust to many outliers.

Where Matching is Used

Summary

  • Template matching scores similarity with SSD, SAD, or brightness-robust NCC.
  • Descriptor matching uses nearest neighbor with the ratio test to reject ambiguity.
  • RANSAC fits a geometric model while discarding outliers.
  • Together they enable stitching, retrieval, reconstruction, and tracking.
