1.
Introduction

What is Image Captioning?

Generating a natural language sentence that describes the content of an image.

  • "man in black shirt is playing guitar."
  • "construction worker in orange safety vest is working on road."
  • "two young girls are playing with lego toy."

Applications

  • Captioning images on social media
  • Assisting visually impaired people through image description
  • Applications in healthcare, smart agriculture, and many other fields

2.
Dataset

Flickr8k

Flickr8k: 8,000 images

  • Train set: 6,000 images
  • Validation set: 1,000 images
  • Test set: 1,000 images

Sample: Multiple Captions per Image

Each image is annotated with several human-written captions.

  • a black and white border collie catches a frisbee in front of an audience
  • a dog jumps to catch a frisbee while many people watch
  • an agile dog catches a frisbee while a crowd of onlookers watches closely
  • a brown and white dog catches a frisbee in it mouth in front of a group of people

Preparing Training Pairs

Each caption is split into (partial caption, target word) pairs for every image, using startseq / endseq markers.

Image → startseq the black cat → next word: sat

3.
Method

Where to Put the Image?

Two strategies for conditioning caption generation on the image feature:

  • Inject: feed the image into the same RNN that processes the words
  • Merge: combine the image with the RNN output after processing the words

Image Embedding

Pretrained CNN backbones extract a fixed-size feature vector from the input image:

  • Inception-V3
  • ResNet

Text Embedding (Seq2Seq)

  • Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)
  • Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
  • Transformer

Decoding: Greedy Search

At each step, pick the single most probable next word.

Fast, but can miss the globally best sentence.

Decoding: Beam Search

Keep track of the top-k candidate sequences at every step, instead of only the single best one.

More expensive, but explores more of the search space and usually produces better captions than greedy search.

4.
Experiment

BLEU Metric

BLEU measures n-gram overlap between the generated caption and reference captions, penalized for length mismatch.

\[\text{BLEU} = \min\left(1, \exp\left(1 - \frac{\text{reference-length}}{\text{output-length}}\right)\right) \left(\prod_{i=1}^{4} precision_i\right)^{1/4}\]

BLEU Score Interpretation
< 10 Almost useless
20 - 29 Gist is clear, significant grammatical errors
40 - 50 High quality translations
> 60 Quality often better than human

Summary

  • Image captioning combines a CNN image encoder with a sequence decoder (RNN/LSTM/Transformer) merged to predict the next word
  • Trained and evaluated on Flickr8k using the BLEU metric
  • LSTM with additive merge and GloVe embeddings gave the strongest results
  • Beam search decoding generally improves caption quality over greedy search
